Build a call QA agent, then put it on the internet

By the end of today you'll have an AI agent that reviews recorded contact-center calls against a QA scorecard and a compliance checklist — running on your own Azure subscription, behind your own web app.

About 2 hours 45 minutes · No prior Azure experience needed

The scenario

Northwind Bank outsources collections and servicing calls to Contoso BPO. Someone has to listen to those calls and score them: was the recording disclosure read, was the customer verified before any balance was discussed, was the agent respectful? Human QA teams can only sample a few percent of calls.

You'll build the agent that does the first pass. It reads a transcript, scores it against the scorecard, cites the compliance rules it broke, and files coaching tasks. Both names are fictional and every call is sample data.

What you'll actually end up with

Three ideas carry the whole workshop. Instructions tell the agent its job. Knowledge grounds it in your organization's rules, so it cites CC-02 instead of inventing a policy. Tools are functions in your own code that the agent can call to read a call or save a score — the agent decides when, your code decides what's allowed.

The steps

  • Step 0 · 20 min Prerequisites An Azure subscription, a GitHub account, and what this will cost you.
  • Step 1 · 15 min Create the project A resource group, a Foundry project and your first model deployment.
  • Step 2 · 10 min Open your Codespace Fork the repo and get a ready-made dev environment in the browser.
  • Step 3 · 10 min Your first agent A model plus instructions. Watch where it falls short.
  • Step 4 · 15 min Add knowledge Give it your scorecard and checklist so it cites real rules.
  • Step 5 · 20 min Add tools Functions it can call to read calls and save results.
  • Step 6 · 15 min Run the web app The front end and API, running in your Codespace.
  • Step 7 · 20 min Deploy from the portal Create a Static Web App that builds from your fork.
  • Step 8 · 20 min Make the live app work Give the deployed API an identity, then require sign-in.
  • Step 9 · 15 min Evaluate Test cases, broken guardrails, and traces.
  • Step 10 · 10 min Delete everything Stop the billing. Do not skip this one.

About money

Everything here runs on pay-as-you-go Azure services. Doing the whole workshop once typically costs well under a dollar in model usage, and the Static Web Apps Free plan costs nothing. The risk isn't today — it's leaving resources running afterwards, which is why Step 10 exists.

If you get stuck

Every step ends with a Checkpoint telling you what you should be seeing, and a If something went wrong section for the usual failures. Beginners most often hit two things: a missing role assignment (Step 1) and a wrong project endpoint (Step 2). If an error mentions permissions or 401, start there.
